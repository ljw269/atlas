# Channel rules

Each section lists only what differs from `voice.md` and `style.md` for that channel.

## Product UI

- **No exclamation points.** No "we." Nothing in first person.
- **Buttons:** start with a verb, use 1–3 words, no articles, no end punctuation, and be specific. "Save changes," not "Submit." "Add member," not "Add a member." "Upgrade plan," not "Click here to upgrade."
- **Destructive actions:** the button names the action ("Delete project"). The confirmation uses "If you continue, [what happens]," says what the person will lose in everyday words ("published sites will go offline," not "unpublished"), and ends by saying it can't be undone. The confirm button repeats the action ("Yes, delete account").
- **Errors:** lead with what's blocked, then why, then what to do next, in plain words. Be specific ("Name must be 50 characters or fewer"). Use one "sorry" only for a real failure, never for validation. No raw error codes.
  - ✓ "Manage your team before you leave Bolt" (names what's blocked: leaving Bolt)
  - ✗ "You're the only admin of Acme Studio" (states a condition and leaves the reader to work out what it blocks)
- **Never blame the reader in an error.** "Couldn't save," not "You forgot to fill in the name." (**must**)
- **First-time and opt-in cards:** lead with the benefit as a real question ("Add AI features to your project?", not "Use Bolt AI"). State the cost or tradeoff plainly ("AI features require extra token usage"). No usage meters or status before there's usage. Step counts read "1 of 2." Buttons answer the headline: "Yes, continue" and "Never mind," not "Continue" and "Skip."
- **Empty states:** a short headline plus a call to action. "No projects yet" + [Create project].
- **Toggles and settings:** describe what the setting does, in present tense. "Plays a chime when Bolt finishes responding." Never start with "When enabled," "When activated," or "If turned on."
- **Formats:** "$25/month," and dates like "Feb 13, 2026."

## Chat (the agent)

- The agent says "I" and calls the person "you."
- When it summarizes work, it says what changed in everyday words: "I added an audio player to your landing page."
- If something failed or isn't finished, it says so plainly and suggests the next step. It never pastes raw error output.
- In discussion mode, it gives recommendations ("You could add…"), not promises ("I'll add…").

## Marketing (blog, social, email campaigns, ads, landing pages)

- Also follow `marketing.md` for the general marketing writing principles: headlines, claims, comparing options, proof, example prompts, and CTAs.
- "We" is fine when Bolt the company is acting.
- Exclamation points are allowed sparingly: one per piece at most, and never as a substitute for strong wording.
- Website heroes and some creative assets may use title case. Everything else uses sentence case, including buttons and other UI-like controls ("Start building").
- Use US spelling, except UK spelling for English copy aimed at regions outside the US.
- Prices in prose: "$25 a month."
- Credit statistics and quotes to the original source. Long-form assets (ebooks, guides) get a bibliography in Chicago style.
- **Cut patterns that sound AI-written:**
  - filler phrases that could appear in any article
  - "This isn't X. It's Y." setups (state Y directly)
  - fragments used as punchlines
  - "What if I told you…" setups
  - inanimate things doing human actions (name the person)
  - "People tend to…" (say "you")
  - lists of three by default (pairs are usually enough)
  - lines that sound like a motivational poster
  - adverbs propping up weak verbs
- Read it aloud. If a sentence sounds rehearsed, rewrite it.

## Customer communications (transactional email, push, in-app messages)

- Emoji: rarely, never carrying the message, and placed at the end of a subject line or push header, or after the first sentence of a message.
- Use quotes for UI names where bold isn't available: Tap "Upgrade plan."
- Use the person's own time zone for their own actions.
