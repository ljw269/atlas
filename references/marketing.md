# Marketing writing

Applies to all marketing writing: landing pages, plan comparisons, campaigns, ads, email campaigns, social, and blog. These are general principles, not rules for one page. The pricing and homepage examples show the pattern (see calibration pairs 004 and 005).

`voice.md`, `terminology.md`, and `style.md` still apply. Marketing mechanics ("we," exclamation points, title case, spelling, prices, AI-sounding patterns) are in `channels.md` under Marketing.

## Headlines and structure

- **Put the stronger idea in the headline.** If the real point is in the subcopy, promote it.
- **A headline invites a comparison or a next step. It doesn't name the page type.** "Explore plans," not "Pricing."
- **Lead with what something is, not what it isn't.** "First-time builders," not "people who have never built before."
- **Name the thing instead of a vague "it."** "Describe an idea. Bolt builds it," not "Describe it. Bolt builds it."
- **Keep a subtitle to two tight lines, and don't repeat "you."** "Create a website or app in minutes. No coding necessary."
- **Keep section headers stable.** Don't change a header when someone selects a different segment or tab.
- **Cut any section that adds scroll without helping someone start or choose.** "New this month" was cut for this reason. A closing section can be just an eyebrow and a header.
- **No buyer or procurement jargon.** "Examples," not "use cases." "Custom pricing," not "Custom." "Priced by usage," not "billed by consumption." Don't say "MSA" or "flexible procurement."

## Facts and claims

- **Say each fact once.** If "free to start" already covers it, drop "no credit card required."
- **A line that sits above every option must be true for every option.** "No credit card required" isn't true of paid plans, so it can't sit above all of them. "Start for free. Upgrade as you grow." is true of every plan.
- **Name limits the way people will hit them.** Don't hide a daily cap.
- **Speed claims name a real time to a working product.** Seconds or minutes are both fine, as long as the claim is true. "Create a website or app in minutes" is one valid example, not the only allowed unit. Don't say "instantly" unless it's actually true.
- **No superlatives or hype.** Marketing has no exception to `voice.md` (Confident: "we know the product is good, so we don't oversell. No superlatives, no hype."). Replace the superlative with the specific claim behind it: "Data protections are written into your contract" and "your data is never used for training," not "Best-in-class security."

## Comparing options (plans, tiers, packages)

- **Lead each option with one short benefit.** Cut filler. Don't leave a leftover line that looks like a link.
- **One benefit per bullet. Put the strongest differentiators first.**
- **Include the value props people actually use to choose,** such as secure data and no daily token limit. Use only value props the product actually has. Don't add one to fill a row.
- **Don't mark a limitation as a benefit.** No checkmark on "Bolt branding."
- **A few honest cons in the entry option make the paid options clearer.** This is a marketing exception to `voice.md`'s "Prefer positive phrasing." It applies only when you're helping someone choose among options.

## Proof

- **Proof must be real.** Credit it to its source (see `channels.md`). An uncited "4.9/5" feels flimsy. Customer stories do that job better.
- **Order proof from most to least compelling.**
- **Quotes still sound like the customer.** A quote edited until it reads as AI-written hurts trust more than it helps.

## Example prompts

- **Example prompts sound like something a person would type,** not a stuffed feature list.
  - ✓ "Create a client dashboard for my design studio that includes files and messaging"
  - ✓ "Build an online store for my ceramics studio that tracks inventory automatically"
  - ✓ "My dog walking business needs a booking site that takes customer deposits"
- **Cut prompt chips or pills that are too specific for most new users.**

## Calls to action

- **Buttons and other UI-like controls use sentence case.** "Start building," "Schedule a 1-1." Title case is only for website heroes and some creative assets (see `channels.md`).
- **Match the main CTA across the page.** If the hero says "Start building," the closing CTA says "Start building" too.
- **Don't put a specific person's name in a customer-facing CTA** unless that's who they'll actually meet. "Schedule a 1-1," not a headline about Mason.
